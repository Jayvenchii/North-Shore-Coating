Driveway sealcoating in Lake Bluff, IL: is your older driveway worth sealing?

By North Shore Coating · Local guide

A common question from homeowners with older driveways is whether sealcoating is still worth it, or whether the driveway is too far gone. It's a fair question. Sealing an asphalt driveway that's structurally sound adds years of life. Sealing one that's failing underneath just makes it black for a season.

This guide is for Lake Bluff homeowners trying to make that call. It covers simple checks you can do yourself, what proper residential driveway sealcoating involves, and when to plan for repair instead.

Three quick checks you can do yourself

1. The water test

Spray a section of the driveway with a hose. If the water beads and runs off, the surface still has some protection. If it darkens the asphalt and soaks in, the surface has become porous. That water freezes inside the asphalt in winter, which is how small cracks grow.

2. The surface check

Run your hand over a sunny section. Loose stone, sandy grit collecting at the edges, or a rough surface where the fine material has worn away all mean the binder is drying out. Those are classic signs a driveway is ready for sealing.

3. The crack check

Look at the cracks you have. Thin, separate hairline cracks are normal on an older driveway and a good reason to seal soon. Cracks that connect into a web, cracks you can see down into, and edges that crumble or sink are signs of base damage and need repair before any coating.

When sealing an older driveway makes sense

Age alone doesn't decide it. An older driveway is usually a good candidate for sealcoating if:

  • It has faded to gray but is still mostly smooth and intact.
  • Cracks are narrow and isolated.
  • There's no sinking, standing water or potholes.
  • Oil stains are on the surface but the asphalt underneath is firm.

In that case, a proper sealcoat slows further drying and keeps water out, which is exactly what an older surface needs. Our checklist of signs your driveway needs sealcoating has more detail.

When to plan for repair instead

If a large part of the driveway shows alligator cracking, or if the same potholes keep coming back, sealer won't solve the problem. Those driveways need patching, resurfacing or replacement by a paving contractor. We only do sealcoating, and if your driveway is past that point, we'll say so in your estimate rather than seal over it.

Why preparation matters even more on older asphalt

Older surfaces are more porous and usually dirtier, so prep makes a bigger difference. Every job we do includes edging along the borders, cleaning the surface and treating oil spots so the sealer bonds to the asphalt. On a weathered driveway, that's often the difference between a coat that wears evenly and one that flakes in high-traffic spots.

Timing and weather near the lake

Sealer needs dry pavement, temperatures of at least 50°F and rising, and about 24 hours without rain, plus whatever the product manufacturer specifies. Near the lake, spring can stay cool and damp a little longer, so late spring through early fall is the realistic window. Sealing in early fall gives an older driveway a fresh layer of protection before winter. After that, plan on a new coat every two to three years.
