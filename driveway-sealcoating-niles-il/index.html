Driveway sealcoating in Niles, IL: protecting your driveway from snow removal

By North Shore Coating · Local guide

Snow removal is one of the biggest sources of driveway wear in the Chicago area. Plow blades scrape the surface, shovels chip edges, and de-icers keep the asphalt wet through freeze-thaw cycles. A sealed driveway takes that abuse on its coating instead of the asphalt itself.

Three ways winter wears a driveway

  • Scraping. Plow blades and steel shovels grind off sealer and loosen surface stone.
  • Salt and melt water. Brine soaks into unsealed asphalt, then freezes and expands.
  • Snow piles. Banks along the edges melt slowly and keep borders wet for weeks.

Winter habits that help

  • Ask your plow service to keep the blade slightly raised.
  • Use plastic-edged shovels where you can.
  • Use only as much de-icer as you need, and sweep up leftovers.
  • Pile snow on the lawn, not along the driveway edge.

Seal in early fall

A coat applied in late summer or early fall goes into winter fully cured. That's the best protection against the season ahead. Sealer needs at least 50°F and rising, dry pavement and no rain for about 24 hours, so don't wait until late October.

Cook County coal tar ban

Suburban Cook County, including Niles, banned coal tar and high-PAH sealants in 2024. Asphalt emulsion is the standard. See coal tar vs. asphalt emulsion.

De-icers and your driveway

De-icers don't usually eat asphalt directly. The damage comes from what they do to water: they melt snow during the day, the melt soaks into openings in the surface, and it refreezes at night. Every cycle pushes cracks wider. Using only what you need, and sweeping up leftovers once the ice is gone, cuts down on the cycles.

Spring after a hard winter

  • Sweep up sand, salt residue and grit.
  • Look for scrape marks and chipped edges where the plow hit.
  • Check the street end for new cracks and loose stone.
  • Do the hose test to see if water still beads.
