Driveway sealcoating in Buffalo Grove, IL: shared driveways and townhomes

By North Shore Coating · Local guide

A shared driveway should be sealed all at once. Sealing only your half leaves a visible line down the middle and leaves the other side unprotected, which lets water work under the edge of your new coat. The easiest path is to agree with your neighbor on timing and cost first.

Agree on three things up front

  1. The date. Both households lose the driveway for about two days in warm, dry weather.
  2. The split. Usually 50/50, or by length if one side is longer.
  3. Who's the contact. One person handles scheduling and questions.

Townhome and association driveways

If your driveway is maintained by an association, sealing may already be on its schedule. Check before booking on your own. If individual owners are responsible, coordinating a few neighbors at once keeps the row looking consistent.

Parking during the cure

Plan where every car goes, including any in the garage. In shade, humidity or cooler weather the cure takes longer. See how long after sealcoating you can park.

Prep

Shared driveways often have one side with more oil staining than the other. Oil spot treatment, edging and cleaning are included in every sealcoating job we do.

When neighbors disagree

Sometimes one household is ready to seal and the other isn't. If the shared section is small, it may be worth covering it yourself so the whole surface is protected. If you can't reach agreement, sealing only your portion is better than nothing, but expect a visible line and more wear along the unsealed side.

Signs a shared driveway is due

  • Gray, faded surface on the high-traffic center strip.
  • Oil stains on both sides.
  • Hairline cracks along the seams.
  • Water soaking in rather than beading.
Tip: send one estimate request with both addresses or the shared address. It keeps the pricing simple for both households.
