Curb appeal

Does sealcoating your driveway help sell a North Shore home?

By North Shore Coating · 7 min read

Buyers start forming an opinion of a house before they ever reach the front door. On the North Shore, where homes in Highland Park, Winnetka, Lake Forest and Glencoe are often judged on condition as much as size, a faded, cracked driveway is one of the first things buyers see, both in listing photos and when they pull up for a showing.

Residential driveway sealcoating won't add a bedroom, but it is one of the fastest, least disruptive ways to sharpen a home's first impression before it hits the market. Here's why it matters, what buyers and inspectors notice, how quickly it can be done, when to schedule it around your listing, and what sealing can and can't fix.

First impressions start at the curb

The driveway takes up a large part of the view from the street and a big share of most front-exterior listing photos. Gray, cracked asphalt makes a home look less cared for, even if the kitchen was just redone. It pulls attention away from the landscaping and the front of the house and makes the whole property look a little tired.

A deep black driveway does the opposite. It frames the house, makes lawns and plantings look greener by contrast, and gives exterior photos a crisp, finished look. Because so many buyers decide which homes to tour based on the first few photos, small exterior upgrades like blacktop driveway sealing can do more work than their cost suggests.

What buyers and inspectors notice

Cracks, potholes and crumbling edges can show up in an inspection report, and they give buyers a reason to wonder what other maintenance was put off. Even small issues can become talking points in negotiations, or line items a buyer expects you to fix or credit before closing.

A driveway that's clearly been maintained sends a simple message: this house has been taken care of. A fresh, even coat shows regular upkeep, and they remove one more item from the list of things a buyer might worry about when they walk the property.

A fast, low-disruption project

Compared with a lot of pre-listing work, sealcoating is quick. It's typically done in a single day, the driveway is ready to drive on in about 48 hours, and there's nothing to move out of the house. You don't even need to be home, which helps if you've already moved out or are juggling showings and packing.

It's also a project with a predictable price. A local sealcoating company can quote by satellite from the address alone, so there's no need to schedule a visit around staging or showings. For sellers trying to get a home market-ready on a tight timeline, that makes driveway sealing one of the easier items to check off.

When to schedule sealcoating before listing

  • One to two weeks before listing photos. That gives the sealer time to fully cure and any light tire marks time to fade.
  • Mind the season. Sealer needs temperatures of at least 50°F, so in northern Illinois the work happens roughly May through September. If you plan to list in late winter or early spring, consider sealing the fall before.
  • Before final landscaping and staging, so crews aren't working around fresh mulch, new plantings and outdoor decor.
  • Leave a weather buffer. A rain delay can push the job back a day or two, so don't book it the day before your photographer.

If you're working with a real estate agent, loop them in on the date. They can line it up with photography and the rest of the pre-listing work so the driveway looks its best on photo day.

What sealcoating won't fix, and a note for agents

Sealcoating improves how a driveway looks and protects it, but it won't fix major damage like large potholes, widespread alligator cracking or sunken sections. Sealing over those problems can make them look better in photos, but an inspector will still find them. If the driveway needs repairs, it's better to find out from your driveway sealcoating contractor before a buyer's inspector points it out.
