How much does driveway sealcoating cost in Illinois? (2026 Lake County guide)

By North Shore Coating · 3 min read

Most residential driveway sealcoating projects range from $250–$550. On a typical driveway that works out to roughly 25 to 35 cents per square foot. Your final price depends on driveway size, surface condition, preparation, and any additional repairs needed.

That's the short answer. The rest of this guide explains what moves a quote up or down, how to estimate your own square footage, and how to spot a price that's too good to be true.

What you're really paying for

Sealcoating is maintenance on one of the most expensive surfaces around your home. National cost guides such as HomeAdvisor put full asphalt driveway replacement at roughly $8 to $15 per square foot once tear-out is included. On a 1,000 to 1,500 square foot driveway, that's about $8,000 to $22,500.

The math that matters: a few hundred dollars every two to three years protects a surface that costs thousands to replace. Sealcoating is how you push that replacement further into the future.

What moves the price

  • Size. The biggest factor. More area means more sealer and more time.
  • Porosity. Weathered, rough asphalt soaks up more sealer than a tight surface.
  • Prep. Overgrown edges, heavy debris and oil stains take longer.
  • Repairs. Wide cracks or potholes need attention before sealing.
  • Borders and detail. Lots of brick, stone or concrete edges mean more hand work.

Large North Shore driveways

Long private drives and estate-style driveways of several thousand square feet don't fit a flat rate. The amount of sealer depends on the actual area and how porous the surface is, and the manufacturer's coverage rates set how far each gallon goes. These driveways are quoted individually after measuring.

Prices that should worry you

Be careful with very low flat-rate offers, especially from door-to-door crews with "leftover material." Those deals often skip cleaning and oil spot treatment, use watered-down sealer, or spray over everything without edge work. The coat looks fine for a few weeks, then wears off.

When comparing quotes, ask each contractor:

  • Are edging, cleaning and oil spot treatment included?
  • What sealer do you use? (It shouldn't be coal tar. Several North Shore towns and suburban Cook County ban it.)
  • How many coats, and how are they applied?
  • Will you tell me if my driveway needs repair before sealing?
  • What happens if it rains?
  • Can I get the estimate in writing?
