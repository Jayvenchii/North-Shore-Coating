Best time of year to sealcoat in Lake County, IL: the temperature and rain rules

By North Shore Coating · 3 min read

The best window for sealcoating in Lake County is roughly May 1 through September 30. Sealer needs air and pavement temperatures of at least 50°F and rising, with nights that stay above 50°F, dry pavement, and about 24 hours without rain. The sealer manufacturer's instructions set the final limits.

The three weather rules

RuleWhy it matters
50°F and rising, nights above 50°FBelow that, curing slows dramatically and the coat stays soft.
Dry pavementSealer won't bond to a damp surface. Morning dew has to burn off first.
24 hours without rainRain on uncured sealer washes it out or leaves it blotchy.

Why the overnight temperature matters

A sunny 60°F afternoon can look perfect, but if the night drops into the 40s, the sealer stops curing and sits soft and damp until the next day. That's how coatings end up tracking onto garage floors or washing out in the next rain.

Rule of thumb: look at the overnight low, not just the daytime high.

Month by month in Lake County

  • April: usually too cold at night. Good time to inspect and book.
  • May–June: season opens. Closer to the lake, it may start a bit later.
  • July–August: the most reliable weather. On very hot days, sealing earlier helps it go on smoothly.
  • September: the smart time to seal before winter, while nights are still warm.
  • October and later: risky. Cool nights and heavy dew slow curing.

Why late-season jobs fail

  • Dew. Long, damp mornings leave little drying time.
  • Leaf tannins. Wet leaves sitting on fresh sealer leave brown stains.
  • Short days. Less sun means slower curing.
  • Tracking. Soft coating gets carried onto floors and walkways.
  • Washout. Fall rain arrives before the coat has hardened.

Why early fall is still the best bet

Sealing in late August or September sends the driveway into salt season with a fresh, fully cured coat. That's when asphalt takes the most damage. Just don't wait until the last warm week.

How long does sealer take to cure?

In warm, dry weather most driveways are ready for foot traffic after about 24 hours and for cars after about 48. Shade, humidity and cooler nights stretch that, and the sealer manufacturer's instructions set the limits. Full hardening continues for days after that, which is why the first week calls for gentle use. See how long after sealcoating you can park.

Planning your date

  • Request an estimate in spring to get on the calendar early.
  • Pick a week without major events at home.
  • Watch the 48-hour forecast as the date approaches.
  • Plan where cars will park for about two days.
