Driveway sealcoating in Highwood, IL: getting it right on a short driveway

By North Shore Coating · Local guide

A short or narrow driveway still needs sealing every two to three years, and it's often easier to plan than people expect. The main questions are where the cars go for two days and how to keep sealer off the garage floor, the sidewalk and the street. Here's what Highwood homeowners should know about driveway sealcoating on a compact lot.

Small driveways wear in concentrated spots

On a short driveway, every car parks in nearly the same place and turns in the same few feet. That concentrates oil drips and tire wear, so the area by the garage and the strip near the street usually fade and crack first. A fresh coat spreads that protection back across the whole surface.

Where to park while it cures

In warm, dry weather, most driveways are ready for foot traffic after about 24 hours and cars after about 48. Shade, humidity, cooler nights and the sealer manufacturer's instructions can stretch that. Before sealing day:

  • Check street parking rules on your block for the two days after the job.
  • Move every car out of the garage too, or it will be stuck.
  • Give neighbors a heads-up if you share a tight curb.

Protecting the garage, apron and sidewalk

Short driveways put sealer close to concrete on every side. Clean edging and hand work along the garage slab, the public sidewalk and the curb keep the coating where it belongs. Every job we do includes edging, cleaning and oil spot treatment first.

Tracking: the most common small-driveway problem

When the walk to the front door crosses the driveway, fresh sealer can get tracked inside. Use another entrance for a day if you can, and wipe shoes for the first week. See how long after sealcoating you can park for the full timeline.

Signs a short driveway is due

Small driveways show wear quickly because every car uses the same few feet of asphalt. Look for:

  • A gray band where tires roll in and out every day.
  • Oil stains that have spread beyond one parking spot.
  • Hairline cracks along the seam with the garage slab or the sidewalk.
  • Loose grit collecting at the street end after winter.

If water soaks in when you spray the surface with a hose, the existing coat has worn through. More signs are in 5 signs your blacktop driveway needs sealcoating.

Small job, same process

A compact driveway doesn't get a shortcut version of the job. It still needs edging along every border, full cleaning, oil spot treatment and an even coat with careful hand work along concrete. On a short driveway, a sloppy edge along the garage or sidewalk is the first thing anyone notices, so detail matters even more.

Tip: take a photo of the driveway from the street and one from the garage when you request an estimate. It helps us see borders, stains and any cracks that need attention.
