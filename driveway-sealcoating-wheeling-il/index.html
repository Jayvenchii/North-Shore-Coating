Driveway sealcoating in Wheeling, IL: crack filling or sealing first?

By North Shore Coating · Local guide

If your driveway has open cracks wider than a hairline, fill them first, then seal. Sealcoat coats the surface but doesn't fill cracks. If the driveway only has fine hairline cracks, sealing alone is usually the right next step.

Setting priorities

What you seeDo this first
Faded color, fine hairline cracksSealcoat
Open cracks you can see intoCrack filling, then sealcoat
Web-like alligator crackingRepair the area, then sealcoat
Potholes or sinkingPatch or resurface first

Why sealer isn't a crack filler

Sealer is a thin liquid. Poured into a crack, it shrinks as it dries and leaves the crack open underneath, where winter water freezes and widens it. Read more in can you seal a driveway with cracks?

Our role

We only do sealcoating. If your driveway needs crack filling or patching first, we'll say so in your estimate so you can have it handled before sealing.

Cook County coal tar ban

Suburban Cook County, including Wheeling, banned coal tar and high-PAH sealants in 2024. See coal tar vs. asphalt emulsion.

Why order matters

Crack filler needs to cure before sealer goes over it. Sealing first and filling later leaves filler sitting on top of the coat, where it looks patchy and doesn't bond as well. Filling first, then sealing, gives an even look and puts the protective coat over the repaired cracks.

Working with a limited budget

If you can only do one thing this year:

  1. Open cracks first. They let water into the base, which leads to the most expensive damage.
  2. Then seal. Sealing protects the whole surface from sun, water and salt.
  3. Then keep the schedule. Reseal every two to three years.

A driveway with no open cracks and only faded color is the easiest case: sealing alone is the right call.
