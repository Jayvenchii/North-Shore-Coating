The ultimate Lake County neighborhood guide to stunning holiday lights: from Lake Forest to Libertyville

By North Shore Coating · 3 min read

From Market Square in Lake Forest to Cook Park in Libertyville, Lake County and the North Shore come alive with lights every December. Here's a guide to traditions worth visiting and neighborhoods worth a slow evening drive, plus how to make your own home part of the tour.

Event dates change every year, so check each village or venue's website before you go.

Lake Forest: Market Square

Lake Forest's annual holiday tree lighting in historic Market Square is one of the North Shore's longest-running traditions, with carolers, Santa and the square glowing around the tree. Afterward, the tree-lined residential streets nearby make for a beautiful slow drive.

Libertyville: Cook Park and downtown

Libertyville lights its village tree in Cook Park, paired with a downtown holiday walk along Milwaukee Avenue. It's a classic small-town evening, with storefronts dressed for the season.

Lake Bluff: the Village Green

Lake Bluff celebrates with a holiday afternoon downtown and the lighting of the orbs on the Village Green, a charming tradition in one of the area's most walkable small downtowns.

Glencoe: Lightscape at the Chicago Botanic Garden

The Chicago Botanic Garden in Glencoe hosts Lightscape, an illuminated trail through the gardens that draws visitors from across the region. Tickets are required and sell out on popular nights, so plan ahead.

Deerfield, Vernon Hills and Winnetka

  • Deerfield hosts a winter lighting celebration in early December.
  • Vernon Hills holds a village holiday light celebration.
  • Winnetka lights up around Dwyer Park with caroling and a visit from Santa.

Neighborhoods worth a slow drive

Some of the most memorable lights aren't at events at all. Older, tree-lined streets in Lake Forest, Highland Park, Winnetka and Glencoe, with large homes and mature trees, often have beautifully lit rooflines and wrapped trunks. Drive slowly, keep headlights low and respect private property.

Tips for a holiday lights drive

  • Go after dark on a weeknight. Streets are quieter and displays are fully lit by 5 p.m. in December.
  • Plan a loop. Pair an event like a tree lighting with a slow drive through nearby residential streets.
  • Bring a thermos. Hot cocoa makes the drive part of the tradition.
  • Be a good neighbor. Drive slowly, don't block driveways and keep voices down.

What makes a home stand out

After a few evenings of looking at lights, you'll notice the homes that stand out share a few things: straight, evenly spaced rooflines, a warm and consistent color, lit trees that glow from base to tip, and a welcoming front door.
